# Contoso Retail — Customer Communication Guide
For all outbound customer service email. Version 3.1.

## Our voice in one line
Warm, direct, and human. We sound like a helpful person, not a company.

## Always
- Open with the customer's first name.
- Apologise **once**, sincerely, in the first two sentences. Never repeat the apology.
- State the outcome plainly in the first paragraph. Do not bury the decision.
- Give one concrete next step with a date or timeframe.
- Close with a single, genuine offer of further help.
- Use British-neutral plain English. Short sentences.

## Never
- Never say: "we apologise for any inconvenience caused", "as per our policy", "unfortunately, due to", "your patience is appreciated", "kindly note".
- Never use exclamation marks in an apology.
- Never mention internal systems, agents, policy clause numbers, approval steps, or ticket IDs.
- Never blame the courier, the manufacturer, or the customer.
- Never promise a timeframe we have not confirmed.
- Never use emoji.

## Approved phrasings
| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "We apologise for any inconvenience caused." | "I'm sorry your order arrived damaged — that's not the experience we want for you." |
| "As per our returns policy..." | "Because it's been more than 30 days since delivery..." |
| "Your refund has been processed." | "I've refunded $189.00 to your Visa ending 4412. It'll land within 5 working days." |
| "We are unable to fulfil this request." | "I'm not able to refund this one, and here's why — and here's what I can do instead." |

## Declines
When we say no, we still give something: an explanation in one sentence, and one alternative. Never decline and stop.

## Repeat customers
If this customer has contacted us before, acknowledge it briefly and without making it awkward: "I can see this is the second issue in a short space of time — I'm sorry about that."
Do not reuse the identical opening line you used with them last time.

## Length
120–180 words. If it's longer, you're over-explaining.
